Your screen is the beginning.

On a piano, find a pair of black keys. C is the white key immediately to their left. Place your right thumb on middle C, then fingers 2–5 on D–G. Keep shoulders loose and wrists comfortable. Try the melody slowly without looking at the app.

01 / TECHNIQUE

Start with the body.

Faber’s primer teaching guide introduces seating, relaxed arms and finger numbers. We introduce these before a melody. The app cannot verify your posture or which finger you use.

02 / PRACTICE

Slow, small, separate.

ABRSM recommends slow practice, hands separately, a gradual tempo increase and counting aloud. Our course uses short phrases, an adjustable metronome and a separate left-hand step. These are teaching practices, not proof of this course’s effectiveness.

03 / FEEDBACK

Hear it. Then recall it.

A 2025 study of 60 non-musicians found meaningful pitch feedback supported immediate motor sequence performance; its advantage did not carry over to a later test without feedback. We therefore offer hints for practice and remove them for the final recall task. This is a design inference, not direct validation.

04 / REAL-WORLD TRANSFER

Real keys matter.

Touch and computer input teach the note layout and sequence but cannot reproduce a piano’s key travel, spacing or resistance. MIDI input lets you practise on physical keys. A teacher can check technique and help you progress beyond this first hour.

Audio: locally generated piano-like synthesis, not sampled acoustic piano. Timing score: intervals between correct notes, with a ±30% tolerance; note duration and technique are not scored. No microphone listening or recording.

What a first hour aims for

Locate C, understand finger numbers, follow a slow pulse, play a simplified public-domain “Ode to Joy” melody, and try a C-major chord separately. Two-hand playing is an optional next step. Repeat lessons whenever needed.
